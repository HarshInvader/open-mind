## 1. Core claim

**One sentence.** A content is experienced when a patch of sensory-format cortex holds it as one settled state, without an interruption by a neuronal OFF-period, for a minimum hold time of about 200-250 ms. The experience is dated back to the start of the input that produced it.

**Mechanism, in four parts.**

| Part | T1's answer |
|---|---|
| What process comes with experience | Recurrent activity in thalamocortical sensory-format cortex (visual, auditory, somatosensory, bodily and interoceptive cortex) that settles into a self-sustaining state and keeps it. "Sensory-format" means cortex whose activity patterns encode things as perceived (colour, motion, faces, touch, body state). It excludes the cerebellum, the basal ganglia and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex. |
| What sets content | Which patches hold a state, and which state each holds. Content is the held pattern, nothing else. Report, memory and action are downstream readers of it. |
| What sets presence | Whether any patch completes a hold. Holding fails when cortex is bistable: an input or a spontaneous event drives a neuronal OFF-period (a down state of near-silence) before the hold completes. Brainstem and thalamic arousal matter only because they keep cortex out of bistability. |
| Time window | Minimum hold time H of about 200-250 ms. While the input keeps changing, the state keeps being rewritten and nothing is held yet. Once a state is held, it is experienced as having begun when its input began. |

**Where the number comes from (and why it is post hoc).** In awake people, TMS-evoked phase-locked activity lasts to 248 ± 12 ms (parietal) and 248 ± 15 ms (frontal). In UWS patients it ends at 167 ± 21 ms and 188 ± 18 ms, and in NREM sleep at 168 ± 9 ms (Rosanova 2018, doi:10.1038/s41467-018-06871-1; open full text read via Europe PMC for this task; the text was deleted after use; ledger C21, C40). Seen and unseen stimuli diverge at about 150-300 ms, earlier with expectation (C35). I chose H to sit between these numbers, so H is fitted, not derived. A fitted H still has to hold across all the domains below, and that is the risk.

**What T1 says about the four shared gaps.**
- *Timing.* Experience exists only once a state is held, so later input can still change it (postdiction, C36). It is then dated to its input's start.
- *Covert awareness.* Behaviour is not part of the condition. A patient is aware when posterior cortex can complete holds, whatever the motor system does (C20, C21).
- *Large activity changes with consciousness intact.* Power, synchrony and hub activity do not enter the condition. Only OFF-periods that cut holds matter, so delta without OFF-periods, psychedelic desynchronisation and local sleep elsewhere leave experience intact (C42, C44, C45).
- *Rich experience while disconnected.* Sensory input and motor output are not part of the condition. Ketamine, REM and lucid dreaming hold states with the gates to the world partly or fully closed (C14, C15, C19).

## 2. Necessary and sufficient conditions

Each condition is stated so that one observation can falsify it.

- **N1 (necessary).** No experience without a completed hold in sensory-format cortex. *False if:* a person reports a content, verified by a no-report read-out, from an interval in which every relevant posterior patch had an OFF-period within each 200 ms. Intracranial recording during a report-verified dream would do it.
- **N2 (necessary).** No experience of a stimulus whose sensory patch has an OFF-period inside its hold window. *False if:* a stimulus followed by a local posterior OFF-period 50-150 ms after its cortical arrival is seen as often as one with no OFF-period (prediction P3).
- **S1 (sufficient).** A completed hold in sensory-format cortex is enough. Frontal activity, report, memory, attention and sensory connection are not needed. *False if:* experience is lost while posterior holds continue unchanged. Example: anaesthetic unresponsiveness with no posterior OFF-periods and wake-like posterior evoked responses, followed by a reliable report of nothing. Ketamine is the near miss: people report dreams there (C14).
- **S2 (sufficient, cortex-only).** Nothing outside cortex produces experience. *False if:* experience is shown without cortex (C55, if it were ever verified).
- **Not required (falsifiable by its converse):** frontal cortex. *False if:* removing or silencing frontal cortex removes experience while posterior holds remain (C08 says this has not been seen).

## 4. Risky predictions no rival makes

"No rival makes it" was checked against the 11 theories in the scorecard and the literature search in section 6. "Rival" includes the IIT/Massimini bistability line, which shares T1's OFF-period ingredient. Where it might make the same prediction, I say so.

### P1. On open data now: recency and spacing of posterior slow waves predict dream reports beyond slow-wave count, delta power, spectral slope and arousal

- **Logic.** The report after an awakening describes what was held just before it. T1 says presence depends on whether the last stretch was free of OFF-periods. The total amount of slow-wave activity should not matter beyond that. So:
  - the time from the last posterior slow wave to the awakening (recency, L) should predict "experience" once count and power are controlled;
  - at a fixed count, slow waves bunched together leave long OFF-free gaps, so the longest gap compared with random placement (spacing, G_z) should predict "experience" too.
- **Why rivals do not predict it.** IIT predicts lower posterior slow-wave activity (C17), and so does the IIT dream study. Neither IIT nor GNWT says the timing of waves matters at a fixed count. A memory account could predict a recency effect. The arousal covariate and the spacing test are there to separate it (see the CRITIQUE).
- **Measure.**
  - Posterior ROI = mean of P3, Pz and P4, plus O1 and O2 where present, filtered 0.5-4 Hz.
  - Slow waves = negative half-waves of 0.25-1.0 s, with amplitude above a label-blind threshold for each dataset.
  - Window = 30 s ending 2 s before the end of the file.
  - L = seconds from the end of the last posterior slow wave to the end of the window.
  - G_z = longest gap compared with 1,000 random placements of the same waves.
- **Data.** DREAM N2 awakenings, "experience" vs "no experience" (delayed report, L2):
  - Zhang & Wamsley 2019: 68 / 37;
  - DeGennaro YoungAdults: 20 / 15;
  - DATA1: 24 / 25.

  That is three labs and 189 cases. LODE is excluded because it has frontal-occipital bipolar channels only, so posterior and frontal cannot be separated.
- **Split.** Leave one lab out (3 folds), with subjects nested inside labs. `check_split` runs on every fold.
- **Baselines to beat.** Model A uses:
  - the 1-30 Hz spectral exponent;
  - posterior 1-4 Hz power;
  - posterior slow-wave count;
  - posterior 8-30 Hz power in the last 10 s (the arousal baseline).

  Model B = A + log L + G_z.
- **Pass mark.** All of these:
  - H1: the coefficient on log L is > 0, with one-sided permutation p < 0.025;
  - H3: ΔAUC(B − A) out of fold across held-out labs has a 95% subject-cluster bootstrap lower bound > 0;
  - the gain vanishes on phase-randomised surrogates.

  Strong pass: also H2, the G_z coefficient > 0 at p < 0.025.
- **Refuted if** the planted check shows power ≥ 0.8 for ΔAUC = 0.05 and either:
  - the H1 coefficient's 95% CI lies at or below 0; or
  - the ΔAUC 95% CI upper bound is below 0.05.

  If power is below 0.8, the result is inconclusive and is logged as such.
- **Caveat I must state.** Scalp EEG sees only large, widespread slow waves. T1's OFF-periods are often local (C44), so the scalp measure is a diluted proxy, and a null weakens T1 less than a null on intracranial data would. I set the pass mark anyway and will not explain a failure away. A failed P1 goes in the negative atlas as "T1-P1 failed".

### P2. Who among unresponsive patients is covertly aware: posterior hold capacity, not global complexity or frontal ignition

- **Claim.** Among behaviourally unresponsive patients (UWS by CRS-R), those who show covert command-following (C20) have TMS-evoked posterior (parietal or occipital) responses that:
  - stay phase-locked to at least 230 ms;
  - show no high-frequency (>20 Hz) suppression within the first 200 ms.

  This holds whether or not their frontal responses show OFF-periods, and whether or not global PCI is above the published cutoff (C41; the cutoff value is taken from Casarotto 2016 at test time, not from memory).
- **Why rivals differ.**
  - PCI and IIT score a global index.
  - GNWT predicts frontal-parietal ignition to the command.
  - T1 says one OFF-free posterior site is enough, and that frontal OFF-periods do not matter.
- **Measure.** The Rosanova 2018 definitions: max PLF time, and the onset of high-frequency suppression, by stimulation site.
- **Data.** Needs TMS-EEG with command-following labels from DoC patients. This is not open (task #20). The Della Bella and Casarotto requests are queued.
- **Split.** Held out by centre.
- **Baselines.** Global PCI, the frontal-site PLF time, and the spectral exponent.
- **Pass mark.** Posterior PLF time predicts covert command-following with AUC ≥ 0.75 in held-out centres, and beats PCI by ΔAUC > 0 (CI excludes 0).
- **Refuted if** covert command-followers show posterior OFF-periods (PLF ending before 200 ms, with high-frequency suppression) as often as non-followers. The test needs 20 or more followers.

### P3. Postdiction and its loss share one clock: a local posterior slow wave inside the hold window erases the stimulus; one after the window does not

- **Claim.** In drowsy waking or N1, where local slow waves occur (C44; Andrillon 2021, doi:10.1038/s41467-021-23890-7), a brief visual stimulus is less often seen when a local slow wave starts over the matching occipital site:
  - 50-200 ms after stimulus onset: much less often seen;
  - 300-500 ms after onset: no less often seen than with no slow wave.

  The early visual ERP (<120 ms) is unchanged either way. The cutoff between harmful and harmless slow-wave latencies should fall at the same hold time that bounds postdiction in the same people. Measure the postdictive window with a feature-fusion or flash-lag paradigm in the same session. The step should lie within 100 ms of the measured postdictive window.
- **Why rivals differ.**
  - GNWT ties loss to failed frontal ignition and does not predict a posterior slow-wave latency step.
  - IIT does not predict a latency step tied to postdiction.
  - Discrete-perception models (Herzog) predict windows but not their abolition by OFF-periods.
- **Measure.** Detection hit rate by slow-wave latency bin, from trial-level hd-EEG.
- **Data.** None open with the needed stimulus timing. This needs a new experiment or a dataset like Andrillon's (availability not checked).
- **Split.** Held out by subject.
- **Baselines.** Prestimulus alpha (C37) and global slow-wave density.
- **Pass mark.** The hit rate for 50-200 ms slow waves is lower than for 300-500 ms slow waves (paired, p < 0.01, n ≥ 20). The fitted step lies between 170 and 300 ms.
- **Refuted if** the hit rate does not depend on slow-wave latency, or if slow waves after 300 ms hurt as much as earlier ones.

### P4 (extra; shared with the bistability line, so not counted as unique). Conscious delta lacks OFF-periods

In conscious states with prominent delta (C42: Angelman syndrome, some drug states) and under anaesthetic ketamine (C14), high-frequency (>20 Hz) power should not drop at the delta troughs. In N3 and under propofol it should. Testable on the Milan set if access is granted (same cohort as C14, so not independent). Frohlich, Toker & Monti 2021 (doi:10.1093/brain/awab095, accepted version read) raise "HADOs not related to up and down states" as one possible resolution. T1 commits to it.

## 5. What T1 does not explain

- **The hard problem.** T1 says which physical events come with experience. It does not say why holding a state should feel like anything. It claims no more than a correlation that would support interventions.
- **Why sensory-format cortex and not, say, the cerebellum or lateral PFC.** T1 stipulates this (C05, C11). IIT at least offers a reason (integration structure). T1 offers none.
- **Intensity and richness.** Why psychedelics feel more intense (C45, C46) is not derived.
- **Unity.** T1 allows separate held patches. It does not say when they form one subject (C03, C04).
- **Attention.** The attentional blink's lag structure (C24) and inattentional blindness (C25) need an attention mechanism that T1 does not have.
- **Neuromodulator puzzles.** The claustrum (C50), zolpidem (C54) and prefrontal acetylcholine (C51, strained).
- **Self, emotion and volition.** C53 and C57 get no specific account.
- **The number H.** It is fitted, not derived from biophysics. A real theory would derive it, for example from adaptation currents (the K⁺ adaptation mechanism Rosanova 2018 discuss).
- **Where experience goes in dreamless N3.** T1 says it is absent when holds fail. It cannot yet tell "absent" from "held and forgotten" without no-report markers.

| IIT 4.0 and the posterior hot zone (doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011465; Siclari 2017 doi:10.1038/nn.4545); Massimini/Rosanova bistability (C40, C41) | Posterior substrate; OFF-periods disrupt consciousness; ketamine stays wake-like | IIT identifies experience with maximal integrated cause-effect structure and gives no time window. T1 drops Φ and says only uninterrupted holding matters. It predicts timing effects at fixed slow-wave count (P1), local posterior sufficiency over global PCI (P2) and a latency step (P3). IIT makes none of these. |
| Delayed "neuronal adequacy" with backward referral, Libet, Wright, Feinstein & Pearl 1979 (doi:10.1093/brain/102.1.193, Crossref title checked 2026-11-18; added after docs/fable_review_1.md) | Experience waits for cortical activity to persist long enough ("neuronal adequacy", hundreds of ms), and is then referred back in time to an early cortical time marker: a minimum hold plus back-dating | Libet's adequacy time is a stimulation-train duration in awake cortex, with no OFF-period presence switch and no sleep, anaesthesia or covert-awareness account. **T1's hold-plus-back-dating is Libet's idea; T1 should be read as Libet's delay joined to the bistability switch, not as a new timing idea.** |
| Discrete perception, Herzog, Kammer & Scharnowski 2016 (doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.1002433, Crossref title checked 2026-11-18; flagged again in docs/fable_review_1.md); Herzog et al. 2020 (doi:10.1016/j.tics.2020.07.001) | Unconscious processing, then conscious rendering at discrete moments, dated back | Herzog gives no neural presence condition and does not link the window to sleep, anaesthesia or covert awareness. T1 adds the OFF-period switch and the cross-domain clock (P3). |
| Attractor / persistent-state accounts: Mathis & Mozer, NIPS 1994 (URL from search, not opened; unverified); Mozer's 2009 Oxford Companion entry (search snippet only, unverified) | Conscious contents = temporally persistent settled states | These are computational models with no neural substrate, no sleep or anaesthesia account and no OFF-period switch. T1 is in effect a neural implementation of this idea plus a presence condition. **This is the closest in spirit, and T1 owes it the core idea.** |
| "Brief conscious moments in up states", raised in Frohlich, Toker & Monti 2021 (doi:10.1093/brain/awab095), citing Baars et al. 2013 (doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00200) and Valderrama 2012 (doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0033477) | Experience in up states, silenced by down states | Mentioned there as one speculative option, with no minimum duration and no predictions. T1 states the minimum and tests it. |
| Bistability as the default state, Sanchez-Vives, Massimini & Mattia 2017 (doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2017.05.015); Storm et al. 2017 (doi:10.1523/jneurosci.1838-17.2017) | Leaving the slow-oscillation regime is needed for conscious-level complexity | A mechanism of state, not a theory of content or timing. T1 uses it as its presence switch. |
| Perceptual retouch, Bachmann 1984 (doi:10.3758/bf03205926) | A delayed nonspecific thalamic signal makes specific cortical content conscious, which explains masking | T1's switch is the absence of OFF-periods for the hold time, not an added thalamic boost. |
| Threshold crossing, Kang, Petzschner, Wolpert & Shadlen 2017 (doi:10.1016/j.cub.2017.06.047) | Awareness at a commitment point, with subjective timing set by it | That account covers decisions, not perceptual presence or sleep. |
| GNWT ignition (doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2020.01.026) | A sustained, all-or-none reverberant state from about 200-300 ms | GNWT's state is frontoparietal and broadcast. T1's is posterior and local, with no broadcast. On P1 GNWT predicts nothing about posterior wave timing; on P2 it predicts frontal involvement. |
| DIT, Aru, Suzuki & Larkum 2020 (doi:10.1016/j.tics.2020.07.006) | Thalamocortical coupling of layer 5 neurons as the switch | DIT could supply T1's cellular mechanism (holding needs apical-somatic coupling). DIT has no hold time and makes no timing predictions. |

**Verdict on novelty.** T1 is not a renamed theory, but it is a synthesis: RPT's locus, the bistability switch from the Massimini/IIT line, and Herzog's discrete windows, joined by one parameter H. What is new is the claim that one hold time links postdiction, masking, OFF-period-based loss of consciousness and covert awareness, and the predictions P1-P3 that follow from it. If P1-P3 fail, T1 reduces to RPT plus bistability and should be dropped.
