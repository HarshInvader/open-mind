# T3. Write-back theory (WBT)

Draft by theory2, task #32, 2026-11-18. Sealed by hash before any test. No data were looked at to write it. The self-score is in `T3_selfscore.md`. Blind scoring is task #35.

**Status in one paragraph.** T3 is a candidate, built first to cover the shared gaps in the scorecard (C36, C20, C42, C44, C19). It claims that what matters for consciousness is the **direction** of influence between fast and slow cortical activity, not their power, their nesting strength, OFF-periods or complexity. A content is experienced when the fast activity that carries it changes the later course of the slower activity around it ("writes back"). Unconsciousness is slow activity running on its own and gating fast activity without being changed by it. Every "explains" mark in the self-score is a retrodiction. Only predictions P1 to P3 can show whether T3 is worth keeping. P1 can be run now on DREAM DATA1, a dataset no agent has opened. My honest prior that T3 is broadly right is low, about 5%. The most likely failure is that the scalp version of the write-back index is too noisy, or is driven by slow-wave shape (see section 7).

**T3 is not T1.** T1 said experience needs a posterior state held for a fixed time of about 200-250 ms without an OFF-period. T3 has no hold time, no fitted constant, no posterior restriction, and does not treat OFF-periods as the switch. A state full of OFF-periods can be conscious under T3 if fast activity still writes back between them. A state with no OFF-periods can be unconscious if it does not.

## 1. Mechanism

**One sentence.** A content is experienced when the fast population activity that encodes it changes the later course of the slow activity in the same and connected cortex, so that the slow activity becomes a running record of what the fast activity carried. Experience at any moment is that running record.

| Part | T3's answer |
|---|---|
| Two layers in every cortical area | A **fast content layer**: local population firing, seen as broadband high-frequency activity (30-45 Hz at the scalp, 70-150 Hz intracranially). A **slow context layer**: the field fluctuations below about 8 Hz (delta, theta, alpha). These reflect dendritic and network state and change over hundreds of ms. |
| Two directions of influence | **Read** (slow to fast): the phase of slow activity gates when fast activity can fire. This is the usual phase-amplitude nesting. **Write** (fast to slow): bursts of fast activity change the timing and course of the slow activity that follows. |
| What sets presence | Write-back somewhere in thalamocortical cortex. When the slow layer is driven only by its own intrinsic currents, it runs autonomously. This happens in thalamocortical bistability, anaesthetic-imposed rhythms and spike-wave seizures. Fast activity is then gated (read) but writes nothing, and there is no experience, however much fast activity there is. |
| What sets content | Which areas' fast activity writes back, and what that fast activity encodes. Content-specific areas supply content-specific experience. |
| Time | The slow layer integrates what is written into it over a time constant, the **write-back lag τw**. Input written within τw of an earlier input lands in the same slow-layer state and changes how the earlier input is experienced. That is postdiction (C36). τw is **measured** per person and per state as the lag of peak fast-to-slow influence. It is not a fixed constant, and no value is assumed here. |
| Level | The spatial extent and size of write-back. It is graded: more areas writing back gives richer and more stable experience. |
| What is not part of the condition | Slow-wave amplitude, delta power, OFF-period count, nesting strength (read), global complexity, frontal ignition, report, memory encoding and connection to the world. |
| Enabling conditions | Brainstem, basal forebrain and thalamic arousal systems (acetylcholine, noradrenaline, higher-order thalamus). They decide whether the slow layer is input-driven or autonomous. They supply no content. |

**A possible cellular route (not part of the claim).** In layer 5 pyramidal neurons, back-propagating spikes can trigger slow dendritic calcium events, so fast output does change slow dendritic state. Dendritic integration theory (Suzuki & Larkum 2020) showed that anaesthetics decouple the apical dendrite from the soma, but it tested the apical-to-soma direction, which is T3's "read". Whether the soma-to-apical direction carries T3's write-back is untested. T3 stands or falls at the population level whatever the cell biology turns out to be.

## 2. Necessary and sufficient conditions

**The write-back index W.** For a channel or contact and an analysis window:
- **F(t)** is the amplitude envelope of the fast band: 30-45 Hz at the scalp (sampling ≥ 250 Hz), 70-150 Hz intracranially.
- **S(t)** is the slow band signal: 0.5-4 Hz in NREM sleep and anaesthesia, 2-8 Hz in REM and wake. The band is set by the state before analysis, never chosen per result.
- **W** is the directed influence of F on the future of S beyond S's own past, at lags from 50 to 1,000 ms. The lag range is wide so that it does not fix τw.
  - Primary estimator: cross-frequency directionality (the phase-slope index between slow-band phase and the slow-band component of the fast envelope; Jiang et al. 2015), signed so that positive means fast leads slow.
  - Accepted alternative: Granger or transfer entropy from F to S.
  - W is reported as a z-score against 200 or more surrogates in which F is circularly time-shifted against S by more than 2 s.
- **R (read)** is the same quantity in the other direction (S to F), plus the standard modulation index.
- **τw** is the lag at which the F-to-S influence peaks.

**Validity gates, required before any W value counts.**
- On IAAFT (phase-randomised, spectrum-matched) surrogates of the raw channel, W must be null (median |z| < 1). Otherwise it measures the spectrum.
- W must survive adjustment for slow-wave shape: peak-trough asymmetry, rise-decay asymmetry and harmonic bicoherence. Non-sinusoidal waves produce spurious directionality (Lozano-Soldevilla et al. 2016; Cole & Voytek 2017).
- A planted fast-to-slow coupling of known size must be recovered at the sample size used.

**The conditions.**
- **N1 (necessary for presence).** No experience during an interval in which W is at or below its null in every cortical region.
  - *False if* a report-verified experience comes from an interval with null W at every intracranial contact. Scalp data can only weaken N1, because a local write-back can be invisible at the scalp.
- **N2 (necessary for content).** A content is experienced only if the fast activity that encodes it writes back.
  - *False if* a content is shown by a no-report read-out (for example optokinetic nystagmus in rivalry) while the area encoding it has content-specific fast activity with null W.
- **S1 (sufficient).** W above null in a cortical region is enough for that region's content to be experienced. Delta power, slow-wave amplitude, OFF-period count, nesting strength R, frontal activity, report, memory and sensory connection do not matter.
  - *False if* there is wake-level W across cortex in a person who reliably reports no experience. One example would be the moment of propofol loss of consciousness with preserved W, followed by a confident report of nothing.
- **S2 (cortex only).** Write-back outside thalamocortical cortex, for example in the cerebellum, is not enough. This is a stipulation, not a derivation (C05).
  - *False if* experience is shown without cortex (C55, if it were verified).
- **Converse claims that make "not required" testable.**
  - **(a)** Experience occurs with high delta power when W is preserved.
  - **(b)** Strong nesting (high R) occurs without experience when W is null.

  Under T3 these two dissociations must both exist.

## 5. What is new

Only these three claims are new. Everything else is borrowed and listed above.

1. **The presence condition is the direction of cross-frequency influence (fast writes slow), not its strength.** I found no published theory that makes this the condition. The nearest, MSTRT, appears to require the opposite dominance. This makes two dissociations obligatory:
   - consciousness with high delta and preserved write-back;
   - unconsciousness with strong nesting and null write-back.

   No scored theory requires both.
2. **The postdiction window is a measured per-person, per-state lag (τw), not a constant.** T1 used a fitted 200-250 ms. Libet used about 500 ms. Herzog allows up to 400 ms. Samaha & Postle tie temporal resolution to the alpha period. T3 says the window follows τw and should shrink to nothing as write-back fails.
3. **Within one sleep stage, including N3, report is predicted by write-back direction at matched delta power, slow-wave count, high-frequency power and nesting strength.** Siclari's power-and-count account, IIT's hot zone and T1's OFF-recency all predict that once these are matched, nothing is left.

## 6. Risky predictions

Each prediction says what T3 claims, why rivals do not claim it, the data, and what would refute it. P1 to P3 are the counted unique predictions. P4 and P5 are extras.

### P1 (open data no agent has used: DREAM DATA1). Write-back predicts N3 dream reports at matched delta

- **Claim.** In DREAM set 16 (DATA1; 10 subjects; 25 channels at 2,000 Hz; 60 s of EEG before each awakening), W is higher before experience reports than before no-experience reports in N3 awakenings (46 experience, 87 no experience per the DREAM records table). This holds beyond a baseline model with:
  - delta power (0.5-4 Hz);
  - slow-wave count and mean amplitude;
  - 30-45 Hz power;
  - the 1-40 Hz spectral exponent;
  - nesting strength R (modulation index);
  - slow-wave shape asymmetry;
  - time of night or awakening index, if present.

  All features come from the last 30 s before the awakening, excluding the final 2 s, are averaged over all EEG channels, and are centred within subject.
- **Size T3 commits to.** At least odds ratio 2 per within-subject SD (log-odds 0.69). Write-back is the presence condition itself, so a small effect would not support T3.
- **Secondary, same data.**
  - **(a)** N2 awakenings (24 vs 25): same sign.
  - **(b)** Without-recall N3 awakenings (36) have W at least as high as no-experience awakenings, and closer to experience awakenings. T3 says memory is downstream of the written record.
  - **(c)** R does not add to the model once W is in. T3 says nesting strength is irrelevant.
- **Why rivals differ.**
  - Siclari-style power accounts and IIT's posterior hot zone predict that lower delta and more high-frequency power carry the effect, so nothing remains at matched power.
  - T1 predicted OFF-recency (now refuted).
  - GNWT has no prediction for N3 slow-wave direction.
  - TTC predicts stronger nesting goes with consciousness. T3 predicts nesting adds nothing, and if anything goes with no experience.
- **Data access.** Figshare doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.24058740.v1, CC BY 4.0, a single 1.6 GB package. That is over the project's download budget, so the test must stream only the last 30 s of each N2 and N3 awakening by HTTP range reads (`experiments/t1_dream/tailzip.py` pattern). The test agent must check the budget before starting.
- **Exposure.** No agent has opened DATA1 EEG (checked by searching `experiments/` for DATA1 on 2026-11-18). I have not seen it.
- **Pass.** Within-subject logistic regression, with p from label permutations within subject. The W coefficient must satisfy:
  - greater than 0 at one-sided p < 0.025;
  - point estimate at least 0.4 log-odds per SD.

  W must also pass the validity gates in section 2.
- **Refuted if**, with the gates passed and a planted check showing power ≥ 0.8 for log-odds 0.69 at this n:
  - the 90% CI of the W coefficient lies inside (-0.69, +0.69); or
  - the coefficient is negative with its 95% CI below 0.
- **Void (neither pass nor refute)** if W fails the IAAFT surrogate gate or does not survive the slow-wave shape covariates. Then the scalp measure is invalid, and that is logged as a failure of the measure, not of the theory.
- **Replication on a second lab.** DeGennaro "Multiple awakenings" (set 9; Rome; 19 subjects; 32 channels at 250 Hz):
  - REM 125 vs 29, slow band 2-8 Hz;
  - N2 177 vs 85.

  The dataset itself is unused, but it comes from the same Rome lab as DeGennaro YoungAdults, which task #16 used, so it counts as partly exposed.

### P2 (not open: DoC data on request). Covert command-followers keep write-back, whatever their delta

- **Claim.** Among behaviourally unresponsive patients (UWS by CRS-R), those who follow commands covertly in an EEG or fMRI imagery task (C20) have resting-EEG W above null in at least one cortical region more often than non-followers. W also predicts covert following beyond delta power and the spectral exponent.
- **Key subgroup.** Patients with delta power in the top half but W above null should follow covertly at least as often as low-delta patients.
- **Why rivals differ.**
  - PCI and IIT use evoked global complexity.
  - Spectral and entropy accounts (EB, and EEG power markers in general) predict that high delta means unaware.
  - GNWT predicts frontal ignition to the command, not a resting property.
  - T1 predicted posterior OFF-free TMS responses.
- **Data.** Della Bella / Paris-Shanghai-Toulouse DoC EEG (requested via queue), or any centre with resting EEG plus a command-following label. Held out by centre.
- **Pass.** W predicts covert command-following with AUC ≥ 0.70 in held-out centres, and ΔAUC over delta power plus exponent has a 95% CI above 0. At least 20 followers.
- **Refuted if** followers and non-followers have the same rate of W above null (difference CI within ±10 percentage points), or if the high-delta, high-W subgroup follows commands at a lower rate than the low-delta, low-W subgroup.

### P3 (new experiment). The postdiction window follows τw, not the alpha period, and shrinks with sedation

- **Claim.**
  - **(a)** Across healthy adults (n ≥ 40), each person's postdiction window correlates with their resting τw (r ≥ 0.4) after partialling out individual alpha frequency. The window is the longest effective SOA in feature fusion, or the flash-lag integration time, measured in the same session.
  - **(b)** Within person, under light propofol sedation that keeps responsiveness, τw and the postdiction window change in the same direction, and the window does not track the alpha-frequency shift.
  - **(c)** At loss of responsiveness, W at rest falls to null before or with the behavioural loss.
- **Why rivals differ.**
  - Herzog's framework has fixed or task-set windows with no marker.
  - Samaha & Postle predict alpha frequency.
  - T1 predicted a fixed hold time.
  - GNWT ties timing to ignition latency, not a resting lag.
  - IIT makes no claim about window length.
- **Pass.** The partial r for (a) has a lower 95% CI bound above 0.2, and (b) has the predicted sign at p < 0.01.
- **Refuted if**:
  - the partial r has a 95% CI including 0 with n ≥ 40; or
  - alpha frequency explains the window and τw adds nothing; or
  - the window and τw change in opposite directions under sedation.

### P4 (extra, open but not unique enough to count). Ketamine and surgical anaesthesia

- **Claim.** In the subanaesthetic ketamine data of Farnes et al. 2020 (Zenodo 4245091, unused), W stays at or above baseline while delta, theta and gamma power change.
- **Claim.** In ds004541 (surgical general anaesthesia with LOC and ROC markers, unused), W falls toward null within 60 s of LOC while R rises, and W returns at or before ROC even if delta stays high.
- **Refuted if** W drops under conscious ketamine (beyond a 0.5 SD non-inferiority margin), or if W stays at waking level after LOC.
- **Not counted** because a W fall at LOC is also expected by many level theories. The unique part is "R up, W down, delta irrelevant at ROC".

### P5 (extra, needs a REM dialogue data set). Which questions reach the dreamer

- **Claim.** In REM two-way communication studies (C19), questions that the dreamer answers correctly are followed by evoked write-back: fast auditory-cortex activity followed by a change in the course of theta. Questions that go unanswered show the fast response without write-back.
- **Refuted if** answered and unanswered trials have equal evoked W at matched evoked fast responses.

## 7. What T3 does not explain, and its weak points

- **The hard problem.** T3 says which physical process comes with experience. It does not say why write-back should feel like anything.
- **The scalp measure may not be valid.** Directional cross-frequency measures are biased by non-sinusoidal waveforms, and sleep slow waves are strongly non-sinusoidal. If W fails the shape gate, P1 is void, and T3 has no open-data test until intracranial data are available. This is the most likely way P1 ends.
- **Why cortex and not the cerebellum** is stipulated (S2).
- **Split brain and unity (C04)** are not addressed.
- **Hydranencephaly (C55)** strains T3, as it strains every cortical theory.
- **Ketamine (C14)** is only compatible: T3 predicts preserved W under anaesthetic ketamine, but the only open ketamine data are subanaesthetic.
- **Frontal versus posterior questions** (C09, C11, C31, C32) get no special account. T3 is neutral on location. That costs it the points GNWT and IIT earn there, and it is honest.
- **The OFF-period link is assumed.** T3 says bistability makes the slow layer autonomous. If intracranial data showed fast-to-slow influence preserved through N3 OFF-periods in people who report no experience, S1 would fail.
