# T2. Anterior-posterior settling theory (APS)

Draft by mind-theory-builder, task #27, 2026-11-18. Not sealed. Not yet scored by anyone else.

**Status in one paragraph.** T2 is T1's spatial rival. It keeps T1's hold time and OFF-period switch but adds one requirement: the posterior state must be stabilised by re-entry from a specific anterior loop. That loop is orbitofrontal, anterior cingulate and inferior frontal cortex, not dorsolateral PFC. I wrote T2 because the main open question in the scorecard is front vs back without report, and because T1 and T2 make opposite predictions on the same DREAM test (P1, hypothesis H4). On the ledger T2 scores 141/250 by the scorecard weighting, or 100/250 harsh, with all 18 "explains" post hoc. That is below T1 on both counts, and it is strained on 6 constraints. Its novelty is thin; see section 6. I keep it as a pre-registered foil, not as my preferred theory.

## 1. Core claim

**One sentence.** A content is experienced when a posterior sensory-format state is held for the hold time of about 200-250 ms while being re-entered by an anterior loop (orbitofrontal, anterior cingulate and inferior frontal cortex), with no OFF-period at either end of the loop.

**Mechanism.**

| Part | T2's answer |
|---|---|
| Process | Posterior recurrent settling, as in T1, plus re-entrant input from the anterior loop sites during the hold. The anterior input carries value and salience and keeps the posterior state from decaying or being overwritten. It is not a report signal and not a broadcast. |
| Content | The posterior held pattern, as in T1. The anterior loop adds affective and evaluative content (C11: stimulating OFC and ACC changes experience). |
| Presence | Both ends of the loop must be free of OFF-periods for the hold time. Frontal OFF-periods alone are enough to block experience. |
| Time window | The same hold time H as T1, about 200-250 ms, dated back to the input's start. |

## 2. Necessary and sufficient conditions

- **N1 (necessary, the claim T1 lacks).** No experience without anterior re-entry during the hold. *False if:* experience is reported, by delayed report or a no-report read-out, from an interval with OFF-periods throughout the OFC/ACC/inferior frontal sites, or after their bilateral removal.
- **N2 (necessary).** The posterior hold, as in T1.
- **S1 (sufficient).** A posterior hold with anterior re-entry is enough. Report, dorsolateral PFC, memory and sensory connection are not needed. *False if:* both are present and a reliable no-report marker shows no experience.

## 4. Risky predictions no rival makes

### P1 (open data now, same sealed analysis as T1-P1, hypothesis H4). Frontal slow-wave recency adds to posterior recency

- **Claim.** In DREAM N2 awakenings, the time since the last frontal slow wave (F3, Fz, F4) predicts "experience" beyond everything in T1's Model B. T1 predicts it adds nothing.
- **Why rivals differ.** IIT predicts no frontal contribution. GNWT predicts frontal involvement, but its signature is ignition to a stimulus and it makes no claim about wave timing (the GNWT prereg in `docs/prereg/` says so). T1 predicts zero.
- **Data, split and baselines.** Same as `PREREG_DRAFT.md`: three labs, leave one lab out. The baseline is T1's full Model B, plus frontal 1-4 Hz power and frontal slow-wave count.
- **Pass mark (T2).** The coefficient on log L_front is > 0 (one-sided permutation p < 0.025), and ΔAUC(B + front − B) has a 95% CI lower bound > 0.
- **Refuted if** the ΔAUC CI upper bound is < 0.03, given planted-check power ≥ 0.8 at ΔAUC 0.05, or if the coefficient's CI lies below 0. A negative coefficient is what Siclari 2018 (doi:10.1523/jneurosci.0855-18.2018) would suggest: steep frontal slow waves with micro-arousals came before successful recall.

### P2. Covert awareness needs an OFF-free frontal response as well

- **Claim.** In DoC TMS-EEG (not open; same data request as T1-P2), covert command-followers have no OFF-periods (no high-frequency suppression, PLF to at least 230 ms) at both a posterior site and a frontal site. Patients whose posterior responses are OFF-free but whose frontal responses show OFF-periods are not covert command-followers.
- **Why rivals differ.** T1 predicts the opposite for that second group. PCI and IIT use a global index.
- **Pass mark.** Of patients with OFF-free posterior but OFF frontal responses, fewer than 10% follow commands covertly, against at least 30% of patients with both ends OFF-free (needs 15 or more per group).
- **Refuted if** the two groups follow commands at the same rate.

### P3. Disrupting the anterior loop inside the hold window erases a percept without report demands

- **Claim.** In intracranial patients, a no-report rivalry or masking paradigm (optokinetic read-out as in C31) with single-pulse stimulation of orbitofrontal or inferior frontal contacts 100-200 ms after stimulus onset shortens or abolishes dominance of the matching percept. The same pulse at 400 ms does not, and dorsolateral PFC pulses do not either.
- **Why rivals differ.**
  - GNWT does not single out OFC and IFG over dorsolateral PFC.
  - HOT would predict changes in confidence, not in the percept read out without report.
  - T1 predicts no effect at any frontal site.
- **Data.** New experiment only.
- **Refuted if** frontal pulses in the window do not change no-report percept dynamics more than sham (n ≥ 10 patients).

## 5. What T2 does not explain

- Everything T1 does not explain (T1.md section 5).
- **Why these frontal sites.** They were chosen to fit C11 and C39.
- **Why frontal re-entry would be needed** when posterior stimulation alone evokes content (C10) and posterior-only signatures predict dreams (C17).
- **The ketamine paradox (C14).** It stays strained until a direct measurement of anterior loop OFF-periods under ketamine exists.

**Verdict on novelty: borderline.** T2 is not a pure rename. No source I found combines a required anterior loop at named sites with a hold-time presence switch. But its spatial half is Mashour/Lee's feedback view and its temporal half is T1. If the blind scorer judges it "T1 + Mashour", I accept that and recommend dropping it. Its main value is as the sealed opposite of T1 on H4.

## Dropped candidate (recorded so it is not re-proposed)

**"Two-gate" theory.** Experience presence is posterior; connection to the world and memory encoding are separate gates. I dropped it as a rename of the connectedness framework in Sanders, Tononi, Laureys & Sleigh 2012, "Unresponsiveness ≠ unconsciousness" (doi:10.1097/aln.0b013e318249d0a7, ledger C15 source). T1 keeps its useful part: it treats connection and memory as orthogonal to presence.
