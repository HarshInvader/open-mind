# T4. Loop-ignition workspace (LIW)

Draft by the theory builder, 2026-11-19. Not sealed. Not scored by anyone else. Predictions are in `T4_PREDICTIONS_DRAFT.md`.

**Status in one paragraph.** T4 keeps GNWT's computational core (a content becomes globally available when a long-range recurrent loop ignites and sustains it) and drops GNWT's anatomical commitment to prefrontal cortex. **As a theory it is not new.** Its three parts are each published: Sergent et al. 2021 already proposed that covert conscious access uses a subset of the workspace without frontal executive areas (the "global playground") and showed bifurcation without a task in scalp EEG; Moutard, Dehaene & Malach 2015 already proposed an integrator that accumulates and then ignites in local recurrent loops; and the idea that PFC holds an index and not the content is in COGITATE's own discussion and in Lau's perceptual reality monitoring. What T4 adds is a set of fixed, falsifiable commitments on one open intracranial dataset where two published accounts from overlapping author groups (bifurcation, and leaky evidence accumulation) make different predictions that nobody has compared. On the ledger my own marks give 136/250, or 100/250 with post-hoc "explains" counted as "compatible". Every "explains" is a retrodiction. My prior that T4 is broadly right is about 15%.

## 1. Core claim

**One sentence.** A content is experienced when a long-range recurrent loop that includes the area encoding that content crosses a bifurcation into a self-sustained high state; the loop can close among posterior areas alone, and prefrontal cortex joins it only as an optional, low-dimensional pointer when a task, a report or a strong stimulus recruits it.

## 2. Mechanism

| Part | T4's answer |
|---|---|
| Process | **Accumulate, then ignite.** Sensory evidence builds up locally by leaky accumulation (as in Pereira, Perrin & Faivre 2022). If it reaches the loop's threshold, reciprocal long-range connections amplify it and the loop jumps to a high state that sustains itself for a few hundred ms. If not, activity falls back to the no-stimulus level. The jump is a bifurcation, so the same stimulus gives a high state on some trials and a low state on others. |
| Which loop | Any reciprocal loop between a content area and at least one other high-level area. For a face, T4 commits to mid-fusiform cortex with lateral occipito-temporal cortex as the minimal loop. Superior parietal and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex are not needed. |
| Content | The pattern in the posterior content areas of the ignited loop. Fine content (identity, orientation, intensity) stays there. |
| Global availability | Other systems read the content by joining the loop, not by receiving a copy. Availability is the property that the loop is in the high state and can be joined. |
| Role of PFC | A **pointer**: a brief, low-dimensional signal (present or absent, category, task relevance) that binds the posterior content to a task set. Lateral PFC joins when a report, a rule or working memory needs it, or briefly for strong stimuli. Its signal does not scale with stimulus intensity or confidence. |
| Presence | At least one long-range loop is in the high state. All-or-none per loop. |
| Graded visibility | The amplitude of the high state in the content area, which grows with stimulus strength and tracks confidence. Presence is all-or-none; clarity is graded. |
| Time | Accumulation takes from about 150 ms to several hundred ms, longer for weak stimuli. Ignition then takes under about 150 ms. Until ignition, later input can still change which content ignites. The experience is dated to the input, not to the ignition. |
| Level (state) | Whether any loop can ignite. Bistable cortex (NREM, most anaesthetics) cannot sustain a high state. T4 adds nothing here to what the bistability literature says. |
| What T4 is not | Not a claim that PFC is necessary (GNWT as usually tested). Not local recurrence alone (RPT): one area's feedback without a long-range loop is not enough. Not a structural measure (IIT): the same anatomy is conscious of a stimulus on some trials and not on others. Not a higher-order theory: the pointer is not a representation of the first-order state as seen, and losing it does not remove experience. Not pure evidence accumulation: a threshold read out from a graded signal is not a bifurcation. |

**Fitted numbers.** The 250-300 ms divergence comes from Sergent 2021 and ledger C35. The fusiform and lateral occipito-temporal sites come from Stockart 2025, which I read before writing T4. Both are post hoc.

## 3. Necessary and sufficient conditions

- **N1 (necessary: a bifurcation).** No experience of a stimulus without a transition to the high state in a loop that includes its content area. *False if:* at a fixed stimulus intensity, single-trial late activity in the content area is one graded distribution in which "seen" trials are just the upper tail, with the analysis shown able to detect a two-state mixture of the observed size.
- **N2 (necessary: long range).** Local recurrence inside one area is not enough. *False if:* seen and unseen trials differ in the content area's late activity, yet no other recorded high-level area shows any coupled change, in patients with dense coverage of the candidate partners. This is hard to show with sparse sEEG; I count it as weakly testable.
- **S1 (sufficient: posterior closure).** An ignited posterior loop is enough. PFC, parietal cortex, report, task and memory are not needed. *False if:* the high state is present in posterior cortex without report and an independent no-report read-out (eye measures, later noticing) shows the stimulus was not experienced; or if removing or silencing lateral PFC abolishes experience while posterior loops still ignite.
- **Pointer (a claim about PFC, falsifiable by its converse).** Lateral PFC's seen-related signal is binary with respect to graded stimulus properties. *False if:* within seen trials, dorsolateral PFC high-gamma scales with stimulus intensity or confidence as strongly as ventral visual cortex does, or if face identity or orientation is decodable from PFC as well as from posterior cortex with single-unit or dense recordings.
- **Low state (inherited from Sergent's model).** On unseen trials the late state of the content area is the no-stimulus state. *False if:* late activity on misses is reliably above correct rejections at matched times.

## 4. How T4 handles the five GNWT problems

| Problem | T4's answer | Honest status |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Report confound: frontal ignition shrinks without report (C28, C29, C31) | Frontal activity is the pointer and the decision joining the loop. It was never the ignition. Without report the posterior loop still ignites. | Retrodiction. This is Sergent 2021's "global playground". Stockart 2025 already showed the posterior half at the level of means. |
| 2. Dreams, covert awareness, ketamine (C14, C17, C19, C20) | Experience needs a posterior loop to ignite, not frontal engagement. | Compatible only. T4 gives no new measurable marker for these states and cannot be tested on them with the data named here. It does not explain why frontal-to-parietal feedback loss tracks anaesthesia (C13) or why prefrontal cholinergic stimulation reverses it (C51); both are **strained**. |
| 3. Postdiction (C36) | The accumulation phase before ignition is a window in which later input changes what ignites. It predicts a longer postdictive window for weaker stimuli. Retro-cueing 400 ms after a stimulus can trigger perception (Sergent et al. 2013), which fits. | Retrodiction, shared with Herzog 2020 and with GNWT's own retro-perception work. **Not testable on Stockart or MetaMosquito**: neither manipulates later input. |
| 4. COGITATE (C39) | No offset ignition is expected: offset is not a new content that needs a new pointer. PFC category decoding at 0.2-0.4 s with no identity is the pointer. Sustained posterior decoding is the loop. | Retrodiction. The pointer reading is the consortium's own suggestion. T4 does not explain the missing sustained posterior synchrony that challenged IIT, and has no stake in it. |
| 5. Graded vs all-or-none | Two variables. Loop state is all-or-none. High-state amplitude is graded and carries intensity and confidence. | Retrodiction. Sergent 2021's bifurcation model already has a fixed low state and a high state whose mean grows with stimulus strength. Stockart 2025 found intensity affects high-gamma on hits and not on misses. |

## 6. Novelty

**Versus GNWT as published (Mashour, Roelfsema, Changeux & Dehaene 2020, doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2020.01.026; abstract read, full text not open).** The review states the hypothesis as a non-linear network ignition with recurrent processing that amplifies and sustains a representation and makes it globally accessible. T4 keeps all of that. It differs in three commitments: PFC is not necessary, PFC's signal is a pointer and not the content, and the high state's amplitude is the graded part of visibility. The GNWT reply to COGITATE (doi:10.1093/nc/niaf037; project notes read) already says offset ignition is not core and that weak PFC decoding may reflect mixed selectivity, so T4's distance from current GNWT is smaller than its distance from the 2020 review.

**Versus T1-T3.**
- **T1** needs a posterior state held for a fixed 200-250 ms without an OFF-period, in one patch of sensory-format cortex. T4 has no hold time and no OFF-period switch, and it needs a long-range loop and a bifurcation. T1 allows graded holding in one area; T4 requires two states.
- **T2** (the nearest) needs anterior re-entry from OFC, ACC and IFC for every experience. T4 says the opposite about necessity: no frontal site is needed. T2's frontal signal is a stabiliser; T4's is a pointer that can be absent. On Stockart's no-report data T2 predicts a frontal signal wherever the posterior high state appears, and T4 predicts none in DLPFC and SPC.
- **T3** makes the direction of fast-to-slow influence the marker and has no ignition. T4 makes no claim about slow activity.

**Verdict.** T4 is a relabelled merge of Sergent 2021, Moutard-Dehaene-Malach 2015 and the pointer reading of COGITATE. I would not present it as a new theory. **What would be new:**
1. The adversarial comparison in `T4_PREDICTIONS_DRAFT.md`: bifurcation vs leaky accumulation vs graded encoding, per region, in intracranial high-gamma with a no-report condition. Stockart 2025 never mentions bifurcation; Sergent 2021 had no intracranial data.
2. A quantitative law neither source states. The candidate T4 offers: the fraction of trials in the high state without report equals the detection rate with report at the same intensity, scaled by one attention factor per person that is fixed across intensities. This is testable only where a no-report read-out exists per trial.
3. A postdiction law: the postdictive window equals the pre-ignition latency, so it lengthens as stimulus strength falls. This needs a new experiment.
4. Any account of C13, C51 and C09 that follows from the loop mechanism and is not added by hand. T4 has none.

## 7. What T4 does not explain

- Why a self-sustained loop state is experienced at all. T4 is a correlate theory.
- State constraints (C12-C21, C40-C49): compatible, nothing derived.
- Prefrontal effects on level (C13, C51) and on the visibility threshold (C09).
- Prefrontal content without report in monkeys (C32).
- Which second area closes the loop for a given content. The face loop is fitted to one dataset.
- The thalamus.

## 8. Prior odds

- **T4 broadly right** (posterior long-range bifurcation is the correlate of seen vs unseen, and PFC is an optional pointer): about **15%**. The estimate is this high only because T4 is a conservative merge of mainstream positions. Main risks: the "bifurcation" is a decision-stage effect that shrinks to graded encoding without report; PFC turns out to carry content at the single-cell level; thalamic loops, not cortico-cortical ones, do the work.
- **T4 is new as a theory:** under 5%.
- **The headline prediction passes** on Stockart: about 20%, with about 40% that the planted check shows the data cannot decide. See the predictions file.
