# Adversarial pass on T1 and T2

Author: mind-theory-builder (the same agent that wrote the theories, so this is a self-critique and not a substitute for the blind scoring task). Each objection is written as the strongest case a proponent would make. It cites only ledger rows (`experiments/constraint_ledger/ledger.csv`) or the scorecard (`experiments/theory_scorecard/`). Each one ends with what I did: **revised**, **answered** or **unanswered**.

## Verdict first

- **T1's strongest unanswered objection (IIT side, with GNWT agreeing):** none of T1's 20 "explains" marks is a prediction. All are retrodictions, and most come from the same IIT-aligned papers the scorecard already flagged as retrodictions (C17, C21, C40, C41). T1's ledger lead (156 vs 119) is therefore just the payoff from having been fitted last. Only P1-P3 can answer this. Until one passes, T1 is not shown to beat anything.
- **T1's strongest unanswered empirical objection (GNWT and PP):** the P1 recency effect could reflect recall, not experience. The DREAM ground truth is delayed report, and C15 shows experience and recall come apart. The prereg guards against this (arousal covariate, spacing test), but no guard on DREAM can fully separate "held and forgotten" from "never held".
- **T2's strongest unanswered objection (IIT):** ketamine (C14) together with C13. Lee 2013 found frontal-to-parietal feedback reduced under ketamine, yet people report vivid dreams. T2 requires that feedback. T2 survives only by an untested auxiliary assumption.
- **Revisions made in this pass:**
  - T1 gained the reset rule (a changing input resets the hold), to cover long postdiction.
  - T1-P1 gained the arousal baseline and the spacing test.
  - T1-P2 dropped a remembered PCI cutoff number.
  - T2 was demoted to a foil.

## T1, settling-window theory

### GNWT proponent

> "Your 'hold' is our ignition moved backwards. A sustained, all-or-none state from about 200-300 ms is exactly the workspace signature. The ledger shows the all-or-none step grows toward frontal cortex: weak signals are lost step by step from visual to frontal cortex (C34, van Vugt 2018). Prefrontal neurons carry rivalry content with no report (C32), and frontal patterns decode awareness without univariate change (C30). Left anterior PFC lesions shift subjective visibility (C09). You mark C09 strained and C32 merely compatible. That is four frontal findings that your theory tolerates but never predicts, while ours predicts them."

- **Answer.** C32 and C30 are compatible with T1 because T1 allows frontal copies of held content. C34 shows nonlinearity at each stage of the hierarchy, and T1 needs only that it is already present in higher posterior areas. C31 and C28 (frontal activity follows report) are explained by T1 and strained for GNWT (scorecard splits).
- **Unanswered part.** T1 has no account of C09 without an auxiliary assumption. T1 also has to show that the all-or-none step is complete in posterior cortex before the frontal step. C34's own data were not re-analysed here.
- **Status:** partly answered; C09 recorded as strained.

### IIT proponent

> "Strip away the new words and T1 is our posterior hot zone plus our bistability account. The marks that carry T1's score come from IIT-group papers: dreams and posterior slow waves (C17, Siclari 2017), OFF-periods in UWS (C21, Rosanova 2018), bistability (C40), PCI (C41). The scorecard itself says these are retrodictions. You even fitted your hold time to Rosanova's phase-locking numbers. And you give up the one thing a theory must do: say why this process is experience. IIT derives its substrate from the properties of experience. T1 stipulates its substrate (C05: you admit 'compatible, stipulated')."

- **Answered in part.** T1 makes predictions IIT does not:
  - timing at fixed count (P1, H2);
  - local posterior sufficiency over global PCI (P2);
  - a slow-wave latency step tied to postdiction (P3).

  These are what separate the two theories.
- **Unanswered.** The ledger score itself, and the "why". I record both. T1 is not ahead of IIT on any evidence that existed before it was written.
- **Status:** unanswered for the ledger comparison; open on the predictions.

### Higher-order (HOT) proponent

> "Experience needs a higher-order representation that the first-order state is present. Visibility and performance dissociate in healthy people at equal accuracy (C27). Anterior PFC lesions raise the visibility threshold more than performance (C09). Your 'held posterior state' does not change between those conditions, yet experience does. Either T1 says the reports are wrong, or it needs a second process, which is ours."

- **Answer.** T1 reads C27 as a dissociation in the confidence and report process acting on a held state, which is why it is marked compatible. The scorecard notes that the prefrontal-cause claim behind C27 failed to replicate (Bor 2017), so its causal part is grade C.
- **Unanswered.** T1 cannot rule out that C27-type conditions differ in experience rather than report. Only a no-report visibility measure could. T1 offers none.
- **Status:** unanswered; C09 stays strained.

### Predictive-processing (PP) proponent

> "Your hold time is not a constant. Expectation moves the seen/unseen divergence from about 300 to about 200 ms (C35). Prestimulus alpha shifts the criterion (C37). That is precision-weighted inference. Postdiction (C36) is simply smoothing: later evidence revises the posterior estimate. PP gets all three without OFF-periods. And on DREAM, a recency effect is what an inference account of recall predicts: the system reports what it last inferred with high precision before waking."

- **Revised.** H is now a minimum hold, and settling is faster when the input matches a prior. That covers C35. I added the reset rule so that long postdiction follows from inputs that keep changing (C36).
- **Answered.** PP has no presence condition for sleep and anaesthesia: the scorecard marks PP silent on most state constraints and calls it "not itself a theory of consciousness". The OFF-period switch is the part of T1 that PP lacks.
- **Unanswered (the recall confound).** The prereg adds the arousal baseline (AR) and the spacing test (G_z). If H1 passes but H2 fails and AR carries much of the weight, the result is reported as "consistent with recall", not as support for T1.
- **Status:** partly answered; the recall confound remains the main empirical risk.

### My own additional objections to T1

- **Scalp dilution.** Local OFF-periods (C44) are invisible at the scalp, so a P1 null is weak evidence. I fixed the pass mark anyway, so T1 cannot use this to excuse a failure.
- **Split brain (C04).** T1 predicts two streams, and the only modern data (n = 2) argue for one. Strained, and recorded as such.
- **The cortex-only claim (C55).** If hydranencephalic children are conscious, T1 falls. The evidence is contested (grade C).

## T2, anterior-posterior settling theory

### IIT proponent (strongest objection)

> "The posterior hot zone predicts dreams within a stage with no frontal contribution (C17). Rivalry switches occur without frontal fMRI activity (C31). Large prefrontal lesions leave consciousness intact (C08). Worst: anaesthetic ketamine reduces the very frontal-to-parietal feedback T2 requires (C13, Lee 2013), yet people report long vivid dreams and their TMS-EEG stays wake-like (C14). T2 is strained on four constraints that IIT explains."

- **Unanswered.** T2 needs the auxiliary claim that ketamine spares re-entry at its specific loop sites (OFC, ACC, IFG) while reducing feedback measured elsewhere. No ledger source tests this.
- **Status:** unanswered. This is why T2 is a foil.

### GNWT proponent

> "T2 is GNWT with the broadcast removed and the hubs renamed. You chose OFC, ACC and IFG after seeing C11 (stimulation effects) and C39 (inferior frontal content in COGITATE). That is post-hoc site selection."

- **Accepted.** T2.md marks both as post hoc and rates its novelty as borderline. Its spatial claim is close to Mashour/Lee (C13 source).
- **Status:** unanswered (novelty).

### HOT proponent

> "If the frontal loop is needed, what does it do? If it marks the posterior state as real, it is a higher-order representation (perceptual reality monitoring). If it does nothing representational, then why would OFC damage remove experience but not confidence? T2 cannot explain C27 (visibility without performance change) without becoming HOT."

- **Unanswered.** T2's "non-representational stabiliser" is not yet distinguishable from a higher-order signal by any measure except P3 (no-report percept changes vs confidence changes).
- **Status:** unanswered.

### PP proponent

> "Top-down re-entry that stabilises a posterior state is a top-down prediction. PP already says perception is shaped by descending predictions. Naming three frontal sites adds a stipulation, not a mechanism."

- **Partly answered.** T2's distinct claims are the site restriction and the requirement that the sites be OFF-free, which PP does not make. Without those, T2 collapses into PP plus T1.
- **Status:** partly answered.

## What a blind scorer should check first

1. Every "explains" mark in `coverage.csv` that cites IIT-group or bistability-line data (C12, C17, C21, C40, C41). Is T1's derivation independent of the source theory, or just a restatement?
2. The C36 mark. Does the reset rule really derive long postdiction, or does it describe it?
3. Whether the scorecard rivals would gain marks under the same leniency I gave T1, for example RPT on C22, C28 and C31.
4. Whether T2 should be dropped as "T1 + Mashour".
