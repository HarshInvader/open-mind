THEORIES
- TOY_Z: Theory Z (a made-up control theory). Canonical reference: none; its full content is given here. Theory Z makes exactly these claims and no others: (1) Conscious contents are held steadily, never switched back and forth many times a second, and suppressed images are never represented in high-level cortex. (2) Awareness of a sound always produces a large late positive brain wave, even with no task. (3) Prefrontal cortex sends feedback to sensory cortex only when a stimulus appears, never when it disappears. (4) Visibility and confidence reports carry over strongly from one trial to the next for many seconds, and prefrontal cortex produces this carry-over. (5) Prefrontal cortex is engaged by sounds only when the listener has a task. (6) Dream experience is marked by lower slow-wave power and higher fast activity over posterior cortex. (7) Frontal cortex plays no part in dream experience. (8) Neurons that are not firing contribute nothing to experience. Theory Z says nothing about the blind spot, damaged visual fields or eye movements.
- AST: Attention schema theory. Canonical reference: Graziano & Webb 2015, Front Psychol, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00500.
- RPT: Recurrent processing theory. Canonical reference: Lamme 2006, Trends Cogn Sci, doi:10.1016/j.tics.2006.09.001.
- PP: Predictive processing as a theory for consciousness. Canonical reference: Seth & Hohwy 2020, Cogn Neurosci, doi:10.1080/17588928.2020.1838467.
- PLACEBO_Q: Theory Q (a made-up control theory). Canonical reference: none; its full content is given here. Theory Q claims only that consciousness depends on the concentration of a particular molecule in the liver. It makes no claims about the brain, sleep, drugs, lesions, stimulation or behaviour.
- IIT: Integrated information theory (IIT 4.0). Canonical reference: Albantakis et al. 2023, PLoS Comput Biol, doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011465.
- HOT: Higher-order theories (higher-order representation / perceptual reality monitoring family). Canonical reference: Brown, Lau & LeDoux 2019, Trends Cogn Sci, doi:10.1016/j.tics.2019.06.009.
- GNWT: Global neuronal workspace theory. Canonical reference: Mashour, Roelfsema, Changeux & Dehaene 2020, Neuron, doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2020.01.026.
